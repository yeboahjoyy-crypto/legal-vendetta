THE PROBLEM

Legal information is powerful — but fragmented.

Legal research can require navigating legislation, judgments, regulations and institutional sources across fragmented information environments.

Fragmented Sources

Researchers may move between legislation, judgments, regulations and secondary material to answer one question.

Context Matters

Legal answers depend on jurisdiction, date, authority level, facts and how authorities interact.

Trust Requires Traceability

AI-generated legal explanations need source visibility, verification and clear uncertainty signals.

AI-NATIVE WORKFLOW

ASK → RETRIEVE → REASON → CITE → VERIFY

01

Ask

Express the legal question in natural language.

02

Retrieve

Find potentially relevant authorities from a structured legal corpus.

03

Reason

Connect rules, facts, exceptions and competing authorities.

04

Cite

Show the authority trail behind the explanation.

05

Verify

Surface uncertainty and keep human verification in the loop.

Verification path

1. Identify jurisdiction and legal issue.
2. Retrieve primary legislation and cases.
3. Check authority, date and amendments.
4. Compare potentially conflicting authorities.
5. Cite the sources used.

This is an experimental prototype. The current demonstration does not perform live retrieval from a Ghanaian legal database.

VISION

Ghana → West Africa → Africa

The long-term vision is an African legal knowledge layer that helps researchers, lawyers, students, institutions and builders navigate authoritative legal information across jurisdictions.

Phase 1 — Ghana

Build a high-quality, traceable Ghana legal corpus and research workflow.

Phase 2 — West Africa

Extend the architecture to additional West African legal systems.

Phase 3 — Africa

Create interoperable legal knowledge infrastructure across African jurisdictions.
